Privacy policy

What happens to your data.

Cycler is built so your rides stay yours. This page says exactly what stays on your phone, what leaves it, and why.

Effective 27 November 2026. Applies to the Cycler app and to this website.

In short

  • Cycler has no account and no ads. Your rides, routes, GPS tracks, photos, names and notes stay on your phone. No health data is ever sent.
  • The app talks to a few free community services for maps, weather and routing. They are listed below, with what each one receives.
  • Once a week Cycler sends anonymous usage stats to a server it runs. They help improve the app. You can turn them off anytime in Settings, Privacy.
  • This website keeps cookie-free visit counts. Unique visitors are counted with a hash that changes every day, and no IP address is stored.
  • A backup is a file you save yourself. Delete a ride or uninstall the app and it is gone from your phone.

This policy covers the Cycler app for Android and this website. It is written to be read, not skimmed past: if something here does not match what you see the app doing, tell us and we will fix one or the other.

What stays on your phone

Rides, GPS points, routes, photos and videos you attach, notes, tags, segments, bike care records and settings are stored in the app's own storage on your device. The developer cannot see them, and they are never uploaded. Android's cloud backup is turned off for Cycler, so they are not copied to your Google account either.

What leaves your phone

Maps, weather and routing have to come from somewhere. Cycler makes six kinds of request, all over HTTPS. Five go to free community services that need no account or API key. The sixth is the weekly usage stats, sent to a server Cycler runs.

Like any web server, the five community services receive your IP address, and they have their own privacy policies. Cycler sends them nothing else and does not add any identifier of its own. The stats server is covered below.

Anonymous usage stats

Once a week, Cycler sends a summary of the previous completed week to a server Cycler runs. It is used only to understand which features are used, how much people ride and where, so the app can be improved and its impact shown to supporters. It is sent over HTTPS.

Report 1: the weekly install summary

  • A random stats ID, created on your phone. It is not taken from the Android ID or the advertising ID, and it is not linked to a name, email or account.
  • The app version.
  • The week, for example 2026-W48. Nothing finer than a week is sent.
  • The number of days you opened the app that week (0 to 7).
  • Rides started, finished and discarded.
  • The total distance of the rides you finished that week, in km.
  • How many times each feature was used, such as GPX, TCX or FIT export, share video or cards, route planning (A to B and loop), GPX import, search, laps, pause, workouts, widgets, offline maps, backups, bike care, and map or routing errors. For Health Connect, only that it was connected, never its data.

Report 2: the anonymous area summary

This report has no stats ID and cannot be linked to report 1.

  • The general area where finished rides started and ended, as a grid cell of about 10 km. It is not the exact point, an address or a place name.
  • The number of rides and km for each area cell that week.

What is never sent

  • Your exact location, routes or GPS tracks.
  • Place names or search text.
  • Ride names, notes or photos.
  • Speed, heart rate or anything from Health Connect.
  • Android ID, advertising ID or device model.
  • Exact times. Only the week is sent.
  • Your IP address. The stats server does not read or store it, and its logging is turned off. Cloudflare's network still sees the IP address in transit, to deliver the request.

Your choice

Stats are on by default. On first launch, and once after an update, the app shows a notice that explains what is shared. It has a Continue button and says you can turn this off anytime in Settings, then Privacy.

Settings, Privacy has a "Share anonymous stats" switch. When it is off, nothing is counted or sent, and stats not yet sent are deleted from your phone.

Where the data goes and how long it is kept

  • It is stored in a Cloudflare Workers and Cloudflare D1 database run by Cycler. Cloudflare acts as the hosting provider.
  • The developer keeps a daily copy on their own computer to make charts.
  • It is kept until you ask for it to be deleted.
  • It is never sold, never used for ads, and never shared with anyone other than the hosting provider.
  • The server code is open source, in the stats-server folder of the app repository.

Deleting your stats

Your stats ID is shown in Settings, Privacy. Email hellokunjcompany@gmail.com with that ID and your stats will be deleted. The area reports have no ID, so they are already anonymous and are not tied to anyone.

Location

Cycler uses your precise location to show you on the map and to record rides. Recording can continue with the screen off and shows a notification while it runs. Your location is not sent anywhere except as described in the table above, in rounded form where the table says so, and in the anonymous usage stats as a grid cell of about 10 km, never the exact point.

Health Connect

If you choose to connect Health Connect, Cycler reads heart rate and weight, only to show heart rate on ride pages and to estimate energy. Access is read only: Cycler never writes to Health Connect. What it reads stays on your phone and is not shared with anyone.

To revoke access, open Android Settings, then Health Connect, then App permissions, then Cycler, and turn off Heart rate and Weight, or remove the app's access. You can also revoke it from Cycler's Settings.

Backups, photos and sharing

Back up everything creates a zip file that you save wherever you choose. It holds rides, routes, photos, segments, bike care and settings. Photos keep their original metadata (EXIF), including where they were taken, because it is not removed. Keep the file as private as the photos themselves. Restore reads such a file back in.

Exports and share cards. Exports (FIT, TCX, GPX), share cards and videos are created on your phone and go only where you send them with Android's share sheet. Once you send a file to another app or person, their policy applies to their copy.

This website

The website is a static site. We do not run accounts or forms on it, and its own code does not set cookies or store anything in your browser. It keeps simple visit counts, described in the next section. Those are separate from the app's usage stats above, which are sent by the app, not by this website.

To work, the page loads a few things from other providers. Each sees your IP address and your browser's details when it serves you a file:

  • Hosting: the site is served by Vercel, which keeps ordinary server logs for security and operation.
  • Fonts: Google Fonts serves the typefaces.
  • Animation code: the cdnjs content network (Cloudflare) serves the GSAP animation library.
  • The planner on the home page: map tiles from OpenFreeMap and the weather line from Open-Meteo load when you reach it. Your search text goes to Photon and the loop's waypoints go to BRouter only when you plan a loop of your own.
  • Visit counts: each page sends a small visit report to our own counter on Cloudflare. See Website visit counts.
  • Links out: GitHub, Buy Me a Coffee, the Tally beta form and our other sites are separate services with their own policies. We do not receive anything from them about you, other than what a payment provider tells us about a donation (see the Terms).

The share card on the home page is drawn in your browser. It is not uploaded anywhere.

Website visit counts

This website counts its visits with a small counter of its own. It is separate from the app's usage stats and their server. It uses no cookies, and the site stores nothing in your browser.

What is counted

  • The page you open, by its path, such as /privacy.html.
  • The name of the site that sent you here, such as google.com, or the utm_source or ref tag in the link you followed.
  • Your country.
  • Your device type (mobile, tablet or desktop), worked out from the width of the page.
  • The family of your browser and operating system, such as Firefox or Android.
  • The day.
  • Clicks on the links to download the app, join the beta, donate or see the source code, and on the share card's download button.

Counting unique visitors

To count each visitor once a day without a cookie, the server combines your IP address and your browser string with a random salt that changes every day, and makes a one-way hash. The IP address and browser string are not stored. The salt and the hashes are deleted after the day ends, so visits cannot be linked across days or back to a person. The hashes are never exported.

This is different from the app's stats server, which never reads your IP address. The website counter reads it in memory to make this hash and does not keep it.

Where the counts go and how long they are kept

  • They are stored in a Cloudflare D1 database. Cloudflare acts as the hosting provider.
  • The developer copies the counts to their own computer to make charts.
  • They are kept until the developer deletes them.
  • They are never sold, never used for ads, and never shared with anyone other than the hosting provider.
  • There is no visitor ID, so there is nothing to look up for any one person.

Beta sign-up form

To join the closed beta on Google Play you fill in a form hosted by Tally (tally.so), which we link to from this site. What you type into it, such as your email address, goes to Tally and to us. We use it only to add you to the beta and to contact you about it, and we do not share it or use it for marketing. Tally is a separate service with its own privacy policy. To have your answers removed, write to us at hellokunjcompany@gmail.com. Google Play also receives your Google account details when you join, under Google's own policy.

How long data is kept

On your phone, data stays until you delete a ride or uninstall the app. Uninstalling removes everything Cycler stored, except files you saved yourself, such as a backup. We hold no copy of your rides.

Anonymous usage stats are kept on the stats server until you ask for them to be deleted. We do not set a fixed retention period.

Website visit counts are kept until we delete them. The daily salt and hashes used to count unique visitors are deleted after each day.

Your rights

  • Turn stats off at any time in Settings, Privacy. Nothing is counted or sent after that, and unsent stats are deleted from your phone.
  • Ask for deletion of the stats already sent. Email hellokunjcompany@gmail.com with your stats ID from Settings, Privacy. Area reports have no ID, so they cannot be tied to you and are already anonymous.
  • Your rides and photos are yours and already in your hands. You can view, export, move or delete them in the app at any time.

Depending on where you live, you may have other rights over your data, such as asking what we hold about you, having it corrected, or complaining to your data protection authority. Write to hellokunjcompany@gmail.com and we will answer in good faith.

The five community services in the table above act on their own account. To exercise rights over what they hold, contact them directly.

Children
